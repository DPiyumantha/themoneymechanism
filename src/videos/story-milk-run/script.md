## Facts and sources

No figures in this episode. The claim is qualitative: stores put staple items (dairy, eggs, bread) at the back, so shoppers pass other aisles first; the coolers also sit at the back for practical reasons, near the loading dock and stock rooms. This is widely reported store-layout logic (per the calendar's Facts column). No chain is named.

**Source (verified 2026-11-08):** David Kestenbaum, "Everyone Goes To The Store To Get Milk. So Why's It Way In The Back?", NPR Planet Money, Aug 1, 2014. https://www.nhpr.org/2014-08-01/everyone-goes-to-the-store-to-get-milk-so-whys-it-way-in-the-back

- Walk-past theory: a BJ's Wholesale Club dairy buyer says they want shoppers to reach the back and see everything else ("building the basket"); a supermarket consultant and Michael Pollan agree.
- Practical theory: economist Russ Roberts says trucks deliver to the back, where the big refrigerated room is (the "cold chain"); BJ's PR called this the primary reason.
- NPR's conclusion: both are true, and no study settles it. The episode's line "So stores put them all the way at the back" is fair. The on-screen note covers the cooler/loading-dock half.
